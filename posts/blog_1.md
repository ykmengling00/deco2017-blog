When I first read the BlaBla Corp brief, the phrase that stood out was "user-centred approach and bespoke features." These are not just buzzwords — they are a direct challenge. If someone could already buy and sell used cars on Facebook Marketplace, Gumtree, or dedicated sites like CarsGuide, what would make a BlaBla community hub worth building?

The brief frames this as a community platform first, not a marketplace. That distinction matters. It is not about transactions — it is about the experience of making a decision that most people find stressful, high-stakes, and full of unknowns. Used car buying is not like buying a book. It is an emotionally loaded, information-asymmetric transaction where trust is everything.

My interpretation: this community hub should serve people who are researching before they buy, and people who are preparing to sell and want to understand their audience. It is a space for shared knowledge, not just listings.

This led me to a core question: What information do buyers and sellers actually need but cannot easily get?

For buyers, the gap is often real-world ownership experience — not just specs and prices, but how a car actually performs over time, common issues, and whether the seller is trustworthy. For sellers, the gap is pricing intelligence — knowing what a fair price is in the current market, not just what they are hoping for. If the community can collectively generate this kind of insight, it is providing something transactional platforms do not. That is where I want to focus.

I also had to consider what makes a community platform different from a regular marketplace. The core functional requirements I identified: users need to browse and search listings, view detailed vehicle information with trusted seller context, and initiate contact without exposing personal details prematurely. Everything else — reviews, discussion forums, pricing analytics — is secondary to those three needs at the prototype stage.

The brief specifies AA accessibility compliance. I initially saw this as a constraint, but thinking about it more, used car buyers include a wide demographic — older users, users with disabilities, people who are not native English speakers. Accessibility is not a box to tick; it is core to the audience. A platform that excludes anyone from making a major financial decision fails at the community mission.

What I am still unsure about: how much community-generated content is realistic to build in a six-week prototype. I can show the structure for trust signals, but the actual community data does not exist yet. That is a gap I need to revisit.
